# Supplemental assessment rubric

This is repository-authored companion material for maintainers and downstream users. The retained Jane Street Socratic Tutor is kept unchanged; this file does not amend or override its instructions and is not automatically loaded by every host.

## Evidence standards

A learner's level describes demonstrated performance on a subskill, not familiarity, confidence, completion of a lesson, or the tutor's impression.

| Level | Observable evidence |
|---|---|
| 0 — Unaware | Cannot identify the concept or recognize a relevant example. |
| 1 — Heard of it | Can recognize terminology or a familiar example but cannot explain the mechanism. |
| 2 — Can follow | Can follow a worked explanation and identify the main steps with support. |
| 3 — Can do with help | Can solve a standard case using prompts, scaffolding, or a reference. |
| 4 — Independent | Can solve an unfamiliar but in-scope case independently and explain the reasoning. |
| 5 — Transfer and teach | Can handle edge cases, critique a plausible alternative, and explain the concept clearly enough to teach it. |

## Promotion criteria

- Require at least two independent checks before increasing a level.
- Include at least one unprompted recall or transfer check.
- Avoid treating repeated variants of the same question as independent evidence.
- Record the evidence and date supporting a level change.
- Keep unassessed subskills explicitly unassessed; do not silently assign a level based on neighboring skills.
- Use the lowest level consistent with the evidence when an answer is ambiguous.
- Lower a level when a later review reveals a material misconception, and record why.

## Scoring dimensions

For each check, record correctness, reasoning quality, independence, transfer, and confidence calibration. A correct guess without a valid explanation is not evidence of independent mastery. A partially correct answer should identify which substep was demonstrated and which remains uncertain.

## Review policy

Schedule review based on the learner's last demonstrated performance, elapsed time, and importance of the concept. A missed review is evidence that the skill may need reinforcement; it is not a reason to shame or pressure the learner. Vary the retrieval prompt while testing the same underlying concept.

## Reporting

A useful progress report shows the current level, the evidence supporting it, the next learning objective, and the next planned review. Never fabricate progress or imply that a short interaction establishes durable long-term retention.
